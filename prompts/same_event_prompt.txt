You are deciding whether two news snippets describe the SAME specific real-world occurrence.

TEXT A (the event as currently tracked):
{a}

TEXT B (a new candidate that cosine similarity flagged as related to A):
{b}

Do A and B describe the same specific real-world occurrence — same action/decision, same core participants acting in the same roles, compatible timing? Two texts can share a topic, a person, or a location and still be DIFFERENT events if the core action/decision is different, or if a different actor performs a distinct new action (even a related one, like a reaction, a separate authority's own action, or a retaliation).

A and B may refer to the same real-world actor or institution through completely different surface wording with no shared words at all — a role or title instead of a name (e.g. "the Foreign Ministry spokesperson" for whoever currently holds that briefing role), a vague reference resolved by context (e.g. "Beijing" meaning the Chinese government), or an institution named instead of the government it acts under (e.g. "the Ministry of State Security" carrying out "the CCP's" policy). Use your own knowledge to resolve these when you're confident; if you cannot tell who or what is actually meant, that uncertainty on its own is not enough to call it DIFFERENT_EVENT — judge on whatever else A and B do share.

Different SEQUENTIAL stages of the same ongoing process (e.g. a policy being considered, then announced, then passed, then implemented; a personnel reshuffle rumored, then formally announced, then the new appointee's first public appearance) are DIFFERENT events unless A and B are clearly describing the same stage. Conversely, a specific fact that differs between A and B — a different named official, sanctioned entity, vessel or aircraft, city, or explicit date — is a strong signal of DIFFERENT_EVENT even if everything else lines up; don't explain it away as just an update.

Do not confuse a sequential stage with a PARALLEL FACET of one still-unfolding deal. A large deal, sanctions package, or investigation is often reported piecemeal over several days: one outlet reveals the financing terms of a Belt and Road project, a different ministry announces its own piece of the arrangement, another report names which specific companies or assets are covered, a third covers a state-owned bank's own confirmation of its role. None of these contradicts, blocks, or undoes any other — they're all just adding detail to the SAME still-forming deal, and that makes them SAME_EVENT, not a new event apiece.

The one test that matters: does B contradict, cancel, block, or try to undo what A describes — or does B just add another detail to the same still-forming thing A already describes? If B is only adding a name, a number, a ministry's own piece, or a bank's confirmation — with nothing in A and B actually in tension — that's SAME_EVENT, even though the specific actor or fact named is different each time. If B is someone trying to stop, reverse, reject, or sanction what A describes, that's the "reaction" case from paragraph 1 and stays DIFFERENT_EVENT.

This exception is narrow in one more way: it only covers a party who is actually PART of the deal/arrangement disclosing their own piece of it (a ministry revealing terms it negotiated, a state bank confirming its own loan) — not a bystander, politician, or commentator reacting to or opining on news they aren't a party to. Two different lawmakers' own separate quotes reacting to the same PLA drill are each their own distinct moment (still the "reaction" case from paragraph 1) even when neither one contradicts the other and both are just commenting on the same underlying news — commentary is not "another detail about the deal," even friendly or agreeing commentary.

Worked examples:
- A: "China's Export-Import Bank unveils new details of a Belt and Road rail financing deal with a Southeast Asian country." B: "Recipient country's finance ministry confirms it will cover three specific rail lines under the same BRI arrangement." → SAME_EVENT — B is just one more detail (which lines are covered) about the exact deal A already describes; nothing contradicts.
- A: "Beijing announces new export controls on rare-earth minerals." B: "Commerce Ministry names the specific mineral categories and license process covered by the same controls." → SAME_EVENT — B names which specific categories are part of the same overall policy A describes; a scale or framing difference between outlets is not a contradiction.
- A: "China unveils a new port financing deal with a Pacific island nation." B: "The island nation's opposition party seeks to block ratification of that same deal." → DIFFERENT_EVENT — B is an attempt to block what A describes, not another detail about it.
- A: "PLA conducts a large-scale naval exercise near Taiwan." B: "Two US lawmakers, from different committees, each issue their own statement condemning the drill." → DIFFERENT_EVENT — each lawmaker's own reaction is a distinct moment of commentary, not another detail about the drill itself, even though neither contradicts the other.
